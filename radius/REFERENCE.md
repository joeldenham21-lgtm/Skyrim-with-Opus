# REFERENCE — what "like the real thing" means

The yardstick is *Into the Radius 2* (CM Games, 2024–). Every domain below lists the qualities the real game has and
that ours must match. Audit rule: for each line, grade **close / partial / garbage**. Anything graded garbage is
rebuilt, not tuned. "Close" means a player who knows the real game would recognise the intent and the feel at once.

## 1. The zone (world and areas)
- A Soviet-era security zone gone wrong: collective-farm villages of log izbas with sagging tin roofs, bus shelters,
  concrete bus stops, transformer substations with lattice pylons, rail sidings with rusted rolling stock, checkpoints
  with striped barriers, watchtowers, military trucks (URAL/GAZ/UAZ) rusted in convoys, a church on a hill, pine and
  birch forest, marsh with reeds and duckboards, radio masts, a dam or pumping station, bunkers and pillboxes.
- The Radius itself is visibly wrong: **floating debris everywhere** — rocks, chunks of asphalt, whole slabs of
  buildings and vehicles hanging in the air, some with grass still on them, drifting and slowly rotating; long
  "trails" of small stones rising; distant black geometric anomalies in the sky; a colossal dark form on the horizon.
- Scale and quiet: long sightlines across empty fields, roads that go somewhere, nothing moving until something does.
  Landmarks readable from afar (the church spire, the water tower, pylons marching off, the substation).
- Interiors are dressed: overturned furniture, papers, bottles, mattresses, hanging wires, propaganda posters and
  hand-written notes on walls (no readable real text needed, but paper on walls), light through broken windows.
- Ground: dead yellow grass, mud, puddles, gravel roads with ruts, moss on concrete, rubble with rebar.
- Anomaly fields are marked by their own debris: burned circles, glassy ground, floating stones circling.

## 2. Lighting and atmosphere
- Overcast, low-contrast daylight most of the time; volumetric haze; sun as a pale disc; god rays through trees and
  broken roofs when the cloud thins; dusk goes amber then a long blue hour; night is very dark with a pale moon.
- Fog sits in hollows and over the marsh; light shafts from the torch are visible in it. Rain and drizzle happen.
- Lights matter: the torch is a warm beam with a hot centre and soft falloff; mimic weapon lights sweep; the base has
  fluorescent tubes and a red emergency lamp; artifacts and fragments glow and bloom; the Tide whites the sky.
- Colour: desaturated, cool greys and greens, rust, the one warm accent of lamp light; film grain; slight vignette.
- The sky: layered moving cloud, the black Pechorsk anomaly, floating debris silhouettes, sometimes shafts of light.

## 3. The base
- A fortified UNPSC outpost: sandbags, a gate, a bunker/warehouse interior with a shop (a catalogue you page through),
  a mission board/terminal, a workbench for cleaning weapons (brushes, oil, rags), a shooting range, sleeping bunks,
  storage crates, a table where you lay out your gear, radio chatter and a generator hum, a clock counting to the Tide.
- Returning to base is relief: warm light, music thins, the Tide siren is loud here, sleeping saves.

## 4. Progression and missions
- Security clearance levels gate what the shop sells and which contracts appear; money from artifacts and missions.
- Contracts read like Committee paperwork: retrieve an item from a named place, plant beacons/scanners, clear a
  location, deliver a specific artifact, recover an explorer's remains, investigate a signal; a main story chain pushes
  deeper into the zone (toward the central anomaly) with narrative notes found on the way.
- The Tide resets the zone on a schedule; you must be inside at the hour or you die.

## 5. Gear and handling
- Guns are physical objects: magazines are loaded round by round from boxes, mags go in the chest rig, reloads are
  manual (mag out, mag in, charge), guns get dirty and jam and are cleaned part by part (barrel, bolt, receiver) at the
  bench; attachments are real parts you mount (rails, red dots, scopes, suppressors, lights, grips, stocks).
- A real Soviet/Russian arsenal plus some Western guns; pistols (PM, TT, APS…), SMGs, AKs in 7.62 and 5.45, shotguns,
  Mosin/SVD, VSS/Val; ammunition types matter (FMJ/HP/AP); armour vests and helmets with classes; backpacks and rigs
  set carry capacity; night vision goggles; gas masks for gas anomalies; probes (thrown) to reveal anomalies; a
  detector that beeps faster near artifacts; a torch; a paper map and compass; a wristwatch for time and the Tide.
- Meds: bandages, medkits, painkillers, stims; hunger is not modelled; bleeding is.
- Weight and load matter: an overloaded explorer is slow and out of breath.

## 6. Entities
- **Mimics**: human silhouettes with weapons; they use cover, flank, talk over radios in garbled Russian, fire in bursts,
  react to your torch at night, are deadly in groups; they carry real guns and armour that you can loot.
- **Sliders**: fast crawling mimics that ambush from cover and cut you.
- **Fragments**: floating glowing orbs (blue/white) that drift toward you and explode; beautiful, chime, deadly.
- **Spawn**: small crawling creatures that hiss and explode or bite in groups near nests.
- **Phantoms/Seekers**: rarer, stranger things — invisible or armoured — that make an area dangerous by their presence.
- Behaviour reads: tells before attacks, sound before sight, patrols that feel like someone is there.

## 7. Anomalies and artifacts
- Electric arcs between floating nodes, fire/heat spouts, gas clouds, gravity sinks pulling debris, reflectors of
  floating glass, "distortions" that warp the air; probes trigger them; artifacts sit near them glowing (orbs, shards,
  strange organics) and are picked by hand and sold at base.

## 8. Sound
- Quiet is the baseline: wind, distant metal creak, a crow, your own footsteps and gear rattle, breathing when tired.
- Radio static and garbled voices from mimics; the fragment's chime; spawn hiss; the electric anomaly's crackle and
  the gas hiss; gunshots are loud and echo across the fields; distant gunfire; the Tide siren; the detector's beep.
- Foley for every interaction: mag clicks, bolt racks, backpack zips, boots on wood, water, gravel, mud.

## 9. Music
- Sparse, ambient, mostly drones and tones with rare melodic fragments; swells with danger; silence often; a distinct
  calm at base; a rising, ominous cue before the Tide.

## 10. Interface
- Diegetic where possible: the watch on the wrist (time, Tide, health), the paper map, the shop as a catalogue, the
  mission terminal as a document; minimal HUD; Committee register in all text.

## Audit method
Each auditor takes one domain, plays the relevant situations headlessly (screenshots at the right times of day,
stepped simulations, sound lists), grades every line above, and writes concrete rebuild orders for every "garbage"
and a fix list for every "partial". Fixers implement them in the owning files. Repeat until no "garbage" remains.
